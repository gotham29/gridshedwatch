Fair comparison

Every method alerts at the same rate, set without looking at any event

Budget: 12 watches and 2 warnings per grid per year, on the median grid. About as often as operators already alert the public in a strained year.

Set on quiet grids: each method’s threshold is tuned on 19 grids that never had an emergency. No event grid is seen.

In practice: this is how any alarm threshold is set. Pick, from past data, the setting that would have alerted about once a month, then keep it fixed.

Without a matched budget, a comparison mostly rewards whoever alerts most often.

The other 20 quiet grids measure false-alarm burden; curves at ¼× to 4× the budget are in the appendix

Mechanics: persistence is fixed; the threshold is found by a scan then bisection until the median calibration grid is within 2% of budget (all 11 entries within 5%). The 39 quiet grids are split in half by the SHA-256 hash of their EIA-930 code: 19 calibration, 20 evaluation. Exact ties in scores are broken with a seeded jitter below 1e-7 (HTM’s likelihood piles up at exactly 0.999); HTM is also scored with 5 tie-break draws, giving 4.4 to 5.0 warnings before onset and 6.0 watches in every draw. Important caveat if asked: the budget is matched on the calibration grids, not on the event grids. On the 8 load-shed grids, realized watch rates range from about 8 a year (Sub-PCA, critical slowing down) to 20 (LightGBM, CUSUM); HTM 15.5. That is why the significance test compares each method with its own alerts at random times (next slide), not with other methods’ raw counts. Budget rationale: ERCOT issued 11 conservation appeals in 2023; CAISO 5 Flex Alerts in 2020 and 8 in 2021. Sensitivity: setting the operating point on all 48 grids instead (still no labels) changes the four leading methods’ watches by at most 0.2.
