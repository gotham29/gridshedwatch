Limits

What this benchmark does not claim

Few events, three storms

Evidence of real warning, not a detection rate to expect in operation. 7 of the 8 come from two storms.

Heat waves

California 2020 is the hardest event: demand tracked its forecast; the shortfall was on the supply side.

Public totals only

Hourly grid totals can’t see lost generation; net generation arrives the next morning.

Budget matched on quiet grids

Alert rates differ on the event grids, so raw counts aren’t compared across methods; chance tests are.

Not a replacement for an operator’s situational awareness

More limits for Q&A: Tier 2 (EEA 3 without shed) is incomplete because public records rarely name the balancing authority; NERC declined to share its list, and a request to CAISO is open. Chronos-Bolt’s pretraining may include EIA-930; every event predates it, so contamination can’t be tested yet. EIA revises past values; the frozen snapshot (Oct 2026) may differ from what was available in real time. Time stamps: alerts use hourly data labelled by EIA-930’s hour; the 1-hour latency check barely changes watches. California 2020: HTM catches it with a watch in 1 of 5 seeds; check the per-event table for the others before quoting them.
