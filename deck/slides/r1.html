“By chance” = the same alerts moved to random times in the same season, so a method can’t pass just by alerting a lot in winter. Listed by watches caught, not ranked: 8 events can’t separate the top methods. Last row: the operators’ own forecast, next slide

Exact numbers: the four shaded methods have Holm-corrected p = 0.0055, the smallest possible, for both watches and warnings before onset. CUSUM passes for warnings before onset only (watches p = 0.25). The z-score catches 5 with chance under 1 but p = 0.14 after correction: its catches cluster in the two storms, and the shared shift accounts for that. Chronos-Bolt catches 6 watches but its alerts are frequent in winter, so chance is 2.6 (p = 0.14), and it gets only 1 warning before onset. Exact 95% Clopper–Pearson intervals for the rounded seed-mean: 7 of 8 is 0.47–1.00; 6 of 8 is 0.35–0.97. Held-out events (5 not used to design the rules): LightGBM 5.0, HTM 4.2, LSTM 4.2, PatchTST 3.2 watches. Median watch lead: HTM 62 h, LightGBM 43 h, PatchTST 12 h, LSTM 11 h (over events caught by at least one seed). Tier 2 case studies are reported separately, never pooled. Why no ranking: the Friedman test on watches without random scores gives p = 0.07; the largest rank gap (5.1) is under the Nemenyi critical difference (5.3); with 8 events and 10 corrected comparisons, no pairwise test can reach significance even at 8 of 8. Dropping one storm also reorders the methods.
