With a day’s real-time delay, no method is shown to beat chance

Each dot is a load shed caught; the line is the median.

EIA publishes net generation the next morning, so live alerts come 11–35 h late. A day late, HTM keeps 4.4 of 8 watches, LSTM 2.0: none beats chance after correction.

Bold: the four methods that beat chance. Paler dots: caught by fewer seeds. Descriptive, not a significance test

Medians of watch lead: HTM 62 h, LightGBM 43 h, PatchTST 12 h, LSTM 11 h, CUSUM 65 h, z-score 107 h, operator rule 140 h. The operator rule warns earliest but doesn’t beat chance: early alone isn’t enough. Why the delay check exists: the benchmark replays every hour as if available the moment it happened. In reality EIA publishes demand about an hour after each hour but net generation only by 11 a.m. Eastern the next day, and the shared rule needs net generation, so a day’s delay is the realistic case. Before Uri, operators’ first public notices came 3–24 h before onset; the four’s first watches came 11–157 h ahead. Before Elliott, no notice preceded onset.
